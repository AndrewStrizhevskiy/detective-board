## A. The hard questions, answered with the numbers of 2026-10-29 (read this first; the list below B is older and may be superseded)

Every number has a file; "n" is always small and the labels were written by an AI (no human review yet). Say the caveat together with the number.

**A1. "This is just vector search with a nice interface. What is different?"**
Honest answer in three parts. (1) Against a bare model the difference is large and measured: on 30 test questions run once (expected ranges written beforehand), behaviour correct 3/30 = 10 % for the bare model, 13/30 = 43 % [27, 61] for the agent, 11 vs 1 questions, p = 0.006 (`docs/spike/results/eval-2026-10-28-test-7b7b197-7857839ed9be-175321.md`). (2) Against our own vector-only configuration (the same quoting pipeline with candidates from the best chunks only) the agent is ahead by 2 questions out of 30, which is noise (p = 0.5); the agent quotes the right sentence in 11/18 answers against 7/18. (3) What retrieval cannot do: the rules (R1 on two quoted facts, with an Explanation), the chronology, the alibi table, the board, every fact with its provenance label and a verbatim quote. Their limit is next.

**A2. "Your rules found nothing. Is the symbolic part real?"**
Real, but fed by hand today. On the automatic graph the rules find 0 of 12 test contradiction cases (0 of 14 on dev) and raise 0 of 8 false alarms on the controls: of 1,700 extracted events only 18 have a person, a place, a day and a clock time, and 3 are exact (all three entered by the team). The two red conflicts in the demo (Garcia at Wisteria Lodge; Lady Brackenstall at the chair and at the window) rest on facts the team entered with a verbatim quote and labelled `curated`, and the finding is `conditional`. The rules are tested (hundreds of unit tests, same answer every time); the bottleneck is extraction, and the plan is fine-tuning the extractor on a server (`docs/team/TRAINING_PLAN_RU.md`), measured against human-checked facts.

**A3. "In the stories the culprit is named at the end. Why does a reasoning system matter?"**
It does not matter for the question "who did it" with the whole text; we measured that this is not what our system solves. Before the reveal (text cut before the explanation, 16 questions, 8 stories) the agent names the culprit in 0 of 16; the control with the full text names him in only 2 of 16, because one quoted sentence rarely holds the name next to the act (`docs/spike/results/pre-reveal-2026-10-28-rescored.md`). The value we claim is verifiability on material where the conclusion is NOT in the text: every statement with its quote, the conflicts between accounts, honest refusals.

**A4. "What did you try that did not work?"** (we show it, it is the point)
(a) An open-ended model judge for contradictions (semantic R3): it said "contradicts" on 8 of 9 controls and found 1 of 14 real cases strictly, so it is switched off and not shown (`docs/spike/results/r3-semantic-dev-judge-r3j2.md`). (b) Choosing the culprit from a closed list of persons: it named an innocent person in 2 of 8 held-out questions, so it stays in an experimental branch (`docs/spike/results/entity-answer-2026-10-29.md`). (c) The sentence index alone did not beat the chunk-only candidates; tuning the candidate list gave +3 answerable questions on dev (optimistic, tuned there). (d) The first evaluation report had a scoring bug (citations stripped from extractive answers); it was found, fixed and the old report marked superseded.

**A5. "Is it honest that precomputation was done elsewhere? Did you train a model?"**
We trained nothing. The extraction of all 596 chunks was run once on the department GPU server with the same open models (qwen3:4b) and the same prompt, 3.2 s per chunk against 180-250 s on the laptop; the organizers allowed precomputing on other hardware. The laptop answers every question offline from the resulting graph and index. The curated facts are human-entered, labelled, and a human re-check is still pending.

**A6. "How do you know the labels are right?"**
We do not, fully. The gold sets (64 questions, 43 contradiction cases, 12 extraction chunks) were drafted by an AI; every table says so. A human review is planned and not done. The metric is mechanical (key-fact coverage >= 0.5 of the expected answer): it undercounts correct answers that are phrased differently. We preregistered the test run (`docs/spike/results/test-preregistration-20261028.md`) and ran the test split once; development numbers are optimistic because the settings were tuned on the dev split.

**A7. "It is slow."**
28 s median per answer on this CPU (18.6-44.1 s, 18 different questions, three series), cold start 44 s, and the CPU sits at 98-100 C, so a long run may throttle (`docs/perf/laptop-20261029.md`). Prefill is about 21 tokens/s and the model only chooses a sentence, it never writes the answer; refusals and rule answers take under 2 s because no model is called.

**A8. "Would a bigger model fix it?"**
For fluency yes; for the problem we measured, partly. qwen3:8b was not better than 4b on the extraction check (entities F1 0.53 against 0.59, claims 0.20 against 0.20, 11.3 s against 6.8 s per chunk on the GPU; `docs/spike/results/gold12-20261027-server-log.txt`), and the constraint of the task is models of at most 8B on a CPU laptop. The weak point is evidence extraction, which a better prompt or a fine-tuned small model may improve; we have not measured a larger model on the answers.

**A9. "Can I run it myself, and is it reproducible?"**
Yes: an offline archive (6.2 GB, sha256 in `docs/perf/clean-install-20261029.md`) installs with the network off and `verify_offline.sh` passed on the author's laptop in an offline network namespace (checksums of 11,291 files, 976 tests, the scripted questions). Not yet verified on a second, different laptop. Decoding is fixed (temperature 0, seed 42); extraction outputs differ slightly across Ollama versions and hardware (the server and laptop outputs for the same chunk were not byte-identical), so the graph comes from the server run only.

**A10. "What is the single most important limit?"**
The extraction of who-was-where-when from running text is weak, so the symbolic layer is starved. Everything we showed with rules uses facts entered by hand. The architecture is sound and measured where it works; the next step is extraction quality, measured against human-checked facts.

## B. Older entries (37), written before the full-corpus graph and the test run; numbers in them may be superseded by section A


**1. What is it, in one sentence?**
A local, CPU-only investigator agent over 20 Sherlock Holmes stories: a small model reads, deterministic rules derive checkable findings, and every answer is a verbatim quote or a refusal. [Text path BUILT and run on the real stack (12 questions, one run); rule path BUILT, run on the real stack only on curated demo facts; `docs/PRD.md`, `docs/HANDOFF.md`, ADR-0009..0011]

**2. Why not just ask ChatGPT or any big model?**
A big hosted model is more fluent and we do not claim to beat it on fluency. Ours must attach a verbatim quote to each claim, drops claims it cannot verify, and runs offline. Pilot evidence on one run: asked "What kind of snake killed Dr. Grimesby Roylott?", the bare qwen3:4b-instruct answers that Roylott "was not killed by a snake" and places him in the wrong book; the agent answers "a swamp adder" with the quote "It is a swamp adder!" (`answers-20261023-v3-BARE-qwen3_4b-instruct.jsonl`, `answers-20261023-v3-qwen3_4b-instruct.jsonl`, g001). That is one question; across the three out-of-corpus and adversarial questions the bare model behaved as required on 1 of 3 and the agent on 2 of 3 (`answers-20261023-v3.md`). We do not claim a general rate. [BUILT as a pilot; a full A/B lift is TBD because `eval/run_eval.py` does not exist. Since 2026-10-27 the agent answer to g001 is the verbatim sentence in about 10 s, `web/replay/real-g001-agent.json`]

**3. Why Sherlock Holmes?**
Public-domain text, rich in testimony, alibis and contradictions, and small enough to check by hand: 20 stories, about 183k words, 596 chunks. Limit: other genres and languages are untested. [BUILT; `data/LICENSES.md`, `docs/PRD.md`]

**4. What exactly runs, and where?**
Ollama on a laptop (i7-8665U, 4 cores, 15 GiB RAM, no GPU), models of at most 8B parameters, fully offline at run time. Two things were computed elsewhere or are to be: the embeddings index (22 minutes on the laptop, once) and the extracted graph (24-30 h of CPU, on stronger hardware, see question 25). [Offline answering path BUILT in `--fake` mode and on the 12-question check; whole product on the real graph DESIGNED; one-command offline start (AC-13) TBD until tested on a clean machine]

**5. How fast is it? The demo looks slow.**
It was slow, and the answer mode changed because of it. Prefill on this CPU is about 21 tokens/s and only the system-prefix cache helps (`docs/perf/ttft-levers-2026-10-26.md`), so the generative answers took 42.0 s to the first token and 59.5 s per question in the 2026-10-23 check (`answers-20261023-v3.md`) and 45.7-140.8 s in the real-API run of 2026-10-27 (`api12-20261027-generative.md`). In the extractive mode the model only chooses numbered sentences from a prompt a third of the size: 8.1-20.2 s per question in the 12-question run (`api12-20261027-extractive.md`), g001 in 9.3 s, and 10.8 s in the recording. Gate refusals take about 0.2 s, rule-first answers 0.3-1.6 s, because no model is called. Language-model composition is no longer in the main demo path. One run each, n=12. [BUILT: measured on the laptop, pilot; finished-system latency on the full graph and rehearsal timings TBD]

**6. Which model do you use and why?**
Decided: **qwen3:4b-instruct for answers**, **qwen3:4b (think off, constrained JSON) for extraction**, **bge-m3 for embeddings**. The numbers are pilot-level: answers rest on a 12-question check (behaviour correct 8/12 for the 4B-instruct, 6/12 gemma3:4b, 9/12 qwen3:8b; `answers-20261023-v3.md`), extraction on 12 labelled chunks (`REPORT.md` section 6), embeddings on 35 retrieval questions (bge-m3 hit@5 0.89 against 0.46-0.69 for the other four; only bge-m3 handled the 5 Russian questions). The decision rationale is in `REPORT.md` sections 8-9 and `docs/HANDOFF.md` section 3. [BUILT: the measurements and the choice; confidence medium-low]

**7. How do you stop it hallucinating?**
We do not claim to stop it. Three layers: (1) the model sees only retrieved evidence blocks and must cite them; (2) a mechanical self-check requires each quote to be a verbatim substring of the cited chunk and to lexically support its claim, otherwise the answer is retried, shortened to the supported part, or refused; (3) a relevance gate refuses before any model call when the retrieved text does not cover the question. In extractive mode (the default since 2026-10-27, ADR-0009) the model does not write the answer at all: it picks a sentence, so the quote is verbatim by construction. In the 2026-10-23 generative check all five answered questions passed the self-check; two needed a second attempt. Residual risk: a quote can be verbatim and lexically close and still not mean what the sentence says (question 14). [BUILT; verbatim-ness is mechanical and guaranteed in extractive mode; whether the chosen sentence is the right one is not judged by a human (pilot, n=12)]

**8. What happens on a question the corpus cannot answer?**
Two paths. The gate refuses without any model call when the retrieved text covers too few of the question's terms: golden question g047 (the Baskerville creature) was refused in 0.01 s with "In these materials there is nothing about this." (`answers-20261023-v3-qwen3_4b-instruct.jsonl`). Otherwise the model may be called and decline: g056 (a fee not in the text) passes the gate, the model chooses NONE and the refusal comes after 15.7-19.4 s (`web/replay/real-refusal-fee.json`, `api12-20261027-extractive.md`). In the older generative path this degraded into a "closest passages" display; the extractive mode refuses plainly (g047, g056 and g057 all refused). The gate thresholds (min_score 0.018, min_support 0.25) are provisional (backlog R-13). In the 2026-10-23 check 2 of 3 out-of-corpus and adversarial questions behaved as required; in the extractive run all three were refused (g057, a false premise, is refused rather than corrected). [BUILT as a pilot (n=3, one run); refusal precision/recall TBD]

**9. What is GraphRAG doing here that plain retrieval cannot?**
Relational and multi-hop questions: who said what about whom, which statements cannot both hold. The graph links persons, places, events and claims to verbatim quotes. Whether this beats hybrid retrieval is what the A/B eval should measure; the result may be negative and will be reported either way. In the 12-question check the graph was off, so that check is not a test of this layer. [DESIGNED; the graph of 8 stories exists and answered the rule questions in the real run, but the graph of 20 stories does not and the lift is not measured: TBD]

**10. How good is the extraction, honestly?**
Weak on claims and relations. On 12 hand-labelled chunks, qwen3:4b scores strict F1 0.55 on entities, 0.31 on claims, 0.18 on relations; the lenient overlap scorer gives 0.51 / 0.48 / 0.45 (`REPORT.md` section 6). 6.6% of its quotes are not verbatim (quote accuracy 0.934; 7-21% across the six models) and are dropped or re-anchored before entering the graph. The gold labels were written by an AI, are not human-reviewed and not exhaustive, so precision is understated and the 12-chunk differences are noise. Prompt v2.1 was 25% faster than v2.0 (`ab-prompt-v2_1.md`); its quality on the gold chunks is not yet scored. [BUILT: pilot measurement. The real raw output was unusable for rules (participants empty in 178/178 events, deny polarity in 98% of claims); a deterministic repair fixes that (ADR-0012), but exact times and days are still missing; quality of the full-corpus extraction TBD]

**11. How do you detect a contradiction? Is it reliable?**
By rules, not by asking the model: the model extracts claims and times, and deterministic rules find the conflict (R1 one person, two places, overlapping times; R3 a witness claim against an established fact). The demo case is Wisteria Lodge: Scott Eccles says Garcia spoke to him in the bedroom "nearly one o'clock"; the police say Garcia lay dead on Oxshott Common "since one o'clock" (gold c001). The rule returns `violated`, marked conditional, because "nearly one" and "since one" both normalise to 01:00 and the story day had to be fixed by hand (`docs/symbolic-layer.md` section 11.1). That is a regression test on the real quotes. In the demo this finding rests on **curated** facts (question 33), not on automatic extraction. On real automatic data R1 and R3 find nothing yet (first real run: 0 exact times, 0 days; question 34). The gold cases are few (question 28). The confidence is the system's own score, not a calibrated probability. [BUILT on curated facts (`web/replay/real-wisteria-r1-curated.json`); automatic extraction feeding the rules DESIGNED, precision TBD]

**12. What is MCP doing in this project? Is it just decoration?**
The alibi and overlap logic is deterministic interval reasoning in one Python module (rules R1-R3), exposed as the `timeline-alibi` MCP server (`check_alibi`, `find_overlaps`, `find_contradictions`, `build_timeline`), which never calls an LLM and reads the graph read-only. The agent talks to it over stdio (a subprocess started with the app; the trace shows `timeline-alibi` and the call time, about 0.1 s); tests prove that stdio and in-process calls return byte-identical results, and the in-process path remains a labelled fallback if the subprocess cannot start. The second, off-the-shelf server (filesystem) was dropped from scope. [BUILT and tested; the 2026-10-27 recordings were made over the graph of 8 stories; re-confirm `/api/health` `mcp.mode == stdio` on the show laptop; ADR-0003, `docs/mcp-contracts.md` 8.1]

**13. How reliable is the alibi check?**
Limited by how well times are extracted and normalised. Vague times ("late in the evening") give `unknown` or low confidence by design and the tool returns `insufficient_evidence` rather than guessing. A statement by a character is evidence of what was said, not of what happened. Normalisation of clock times, day parts and "since/nearly" cues exists (`extract/normalise.py`) and is tested on real passages, not on the whole corpus. [DESIGNED; precision/recall TBD]

**14. Prompt injection: what if a story contains "ignore previous instructions"?**
Corpus text is data: it goes only into delimited evidence blocks, never into the system prompt, and the UI renders it as plain text. Extraction-time injection is an open item in `docs/security.md`. No claim of immunity: small models can still be steered. [DESIGNED; the injected-chunk test result TBD]

**15. Is the file tool safe? Can the model read my disk?**
The off-the-shelf filesystem server was cut from scope (ROADMAP cut order); the only tool server is our own `timeline-alibi`, which reads the facts store read-only and exposes no write or file-reading tool. [BUILT as a design decision; the claim "no file access" is verified by the tool list in `docs/mcp-contracts.md`, not by a penetration test]

**16. How will you measure quality? What are the numbers?**
What exists: the 12-question answer check (behaviour correct 8/12, answered and verified 5 of 10 answerable, gold chunk cited 5/10, gold span cited 4/10; first token 42.0 s), the retrieval pilot (hybrid RRF k=30, bge-m3: hit@5 0.97 on 35 questions, tuned on the same questions so optimistic; the gold chunk is in the top 4 for 70 of 86 answerable questions = 81%), and the extraction pilot (question 10). What does not exist: the four-configuration A/B runner (`eval/run_eval.py`), contradiction precision/recall on the real graph. Failures are listed, not removed. A difference of one question is noise. [BUILT: pilot numbers in `answers-20261023-v3.md`, `arms-20261023-080849.md`, `evidence-20261023-093201.md`; full eval TBD]

**17. Why is the demo in English only?**
The corpus is English and Russian is optional. With bge-m3 Russian retrieval works (hit@5 1.00 on 5 questions, `REPORT.md` section 4), and a Russian question is now translated to English by a local model before anything else (optional step `translate_questions`, `AgentConfig.translate_model`, default qwen3:4b-instruct; adds about 5-15 s, only for Russian questions). Retrieval, the gate and the extractive selection then run on the English text, the quotes stay English and the answer says `Question translated to English: "..."`. Quality is unmeasured (n=1-7 questions, no real run yet); a failed translation is an honest refusal. [BUILT; translation quality NOT measured]

**18. Is the demo real or staged?**
Every step says which. The graph and the model answers are real; parts are recordings, labelled `RECORDING` on screen and in speech: the bare-model transcript (a real run from 2026-10-23, kept verbatim), and any stored run. Live: the g001 agent question (extractive, about 10 s), the rule finding on curated facts and the MCP call, the gate refusal, and the `rg` quote checks. **21. What does "neuro-symbolic" mean here, concretely?**
Two parts with different guarantees. The model reads text and extracts persons, places, times and claims with verbatim quotes: probabilistic, label `extracted`, with confidence. Explicit rules then reason over those facts: R1 one person cannot be in two places in overlapping intervals; R2 event order is transitive and acyclic; R3 a witness claim incompatible with an established fact. Output is deterministic, label `derived` (or `conditional`), with an explanation listing the facts and quotes used. [Rules and explanations BUILT as code with 70 deterministic tests on hand-built and real-quote cases; on a real extracted graph DESIGNED; ADR-0004, `docs/symbolic-layer.md`]

**22. If the model extracts a wrong fact, does the rule layer catch it?**
No. Extraction errors propagate: a misread time or a mis-attributed claim yields a wrong but well-explained finding. With claims at strict F1 0.31 (question 10), this is the main risk (the curated demo facts avoid it for the demo case, with the caveats of question 33), and R1 and R3 consume exactly those claims. Three limits on the damage: every input in the explanation shows its quote, so a human can check it; a finding resting on a low-confidence input is marked `conditional` (as the Wisteria case is); the rules are tested separately from extraction. We claim "follows from the stated facts", not "true in the story". [DESIGNED; propagation rate on the real graph TBD]

**23. What is actually proven, and how do you know the rules are right?**
Only that the conclusion follows from the facts given, by explicit code. The rules are a pure Python module tested on hand-built graphs with hand-worked answers, including boundary cases (touching intervals, cycles, unknown times), plus regression tests on real quotes. The MCP tool wraps the same module. Rules are correct relative to their written definition, not to every reading of a novel. [BUILT: tests pass; correctness against real extracted data TBD]

**24. How do you measure the contradiction finder, and is it better than asking the model?**
Separately from answer quality: precision and recall on the gold contradictions (`eval/golden/contradictions.jsonl`, 43 cases including consistent controls), and the false-positive rate on controls. Not measured yet on the real graph; whether it beats a model judging pairs directly is not measured, and no number is stated. [TBD]

**25. What does "verified" mean? Is a verified answer true?**
No. "Verified" means two mechanical things: every quote is a verbatim substring of the chunk it cites, and the claim is lexically supported by the quote (shared content words). It does not mean the claim is true, that the quote is the best evidence, or that the model understood it. Example from the check: g028 passed the self-check on its second attempt, after the first claim was rejected as "not supported by its quote". Also, a character's statement is evidence of what was said, not of what happened. [BUILT; limits stated in `answers-20261023-v3.md` caveats]

**26. Why were only 5 of 10 answerable questions answered and verified?**
From the per-question table (`answers-20261023-v3-qwen3_4b-instruct.jsonl`): g001, g028, g019, g022, g038 answered and verified. Of the other five: g007 (the model declined although the gold chunk was retrieved), g032 (the gold chunk was not retrieved, model declined), g016 (partial: one claim uncited, one not supported by its quote), g057 (false premise: model declined instead of correcting it), g062 (Russian, refused by the gate in that run, before question translation existed). So the misses are mostly retrieval (the gold chunk is in the top 4 for 81% of answerable questions, but the evidence needed is inside the prompt for only 66-69%, `evidence-20261023-093201.md`) and a cautious 4B model. n=12, one run, temperature 0: one question is 8 points. Strictness is a feature (refuse rather than invent) but it costs coverage: mean coverage on answerable questions is 0.33 (crude proxy). [BUILT: pilot]

**27. The gold sets were written by an AI. Why trust the numbers?**
Partly we do not. The golden questions, the contradiction cases and the extraction labels were written by a Claude agent and were not reviewed by a human (`eval/golden/README.md`); a human spot-check of 10 questions and 4 extraction chunks is on the list. Consequences: precision numbers are underestimates (gold is not exhaustive), recall is more trustworthy, and some labels are debatable (c042 is flagged low-confidence; g027 and c042 disagree). The retrieval parameters were tuned on the same questions they are scored on, so 0.97 is optimistic. Every number in this project is therefore a pilot number. [BUILT: caveats stated in every metric file; human review TBD]

**28. Only 4 genuine R1 cases? Is that a demonstration or a result?**
A demonstration. The corpus has only 4 genuine R1 (person in two places) and 3 R2 (order cycle) cases, and R3 dominates (`docs/HANDOFF.md` section 5). The demo shows one of the four, with the case chosen because the text itself calls the conflict "perfectly impossible"; no recall or precision for R1 can be claimed from four cases, and we do not. [BUILT: counts; precision/recall TBD]

**29. Why is McCarthy vs Crowder not flagged as a contradiction?**
Because it is not a conflict of observed facts (gold c042, a consistent control). Both agree James went the same way with a gun a few minutes after his father; they differ on whether he was deliberately following, which the gamekeeper gives as "his belief" and James denies as intent. Intent is not a checkable proposition, so rule R3 correctly returns no violation (regression test on the real quotes, `docs/symbolic-layer.md` section 11.1). Honest caveat: c042 is itself low-confidence, and the golden question g027 treats the same pair as a conflict between accounts; whether an inference against a denial counts as a contradiction is a labelling decision for a human. The rules were not run on the real extracted graph for this case. [DESIGNED]

**30. Why a 4B model? Is that not too small?**
Two reasons: the hardware limit and the measurements. The laptop has no GPU and 15 GiB RAM; the PRD sets a limit of 8B. qwen3:4b-instruct against qwen3:8b on the same 12 questions: behaviour correct 8/12 against 9/12, answered and verified 5/10 against 5/10, first token 42.0 s against 101.1 s, resident memory about 3.2 GiB against 6.0 GiB, and sustained 7-8B load throttled the CPU to 96 C (`REPORT.md` sections 2-3, 9). The 8B is at most one question better, which is noise at n=12, and 2.4 times slower. It is too small to reason well: that is why reasoning is left to rules and the model only reads, extracts and phrases; and its weakness shows in claims (F1 0.31) and cautious refusals (question 26). A stronger model would likely help and is not measured. [BUILT: measurements, pilot]

**31. Where was the extraction done? Did you cheat the CPU limit?**
Answering runs on the laptop. Extracting 596 chunks takes about 179.7 s per chunk with qwen3:4b, projected at 29.7 h (`REPORT.md` section 7; 24-30 h in `docs/HANDOFF.md`), which does not fit before the show, so the extraction is precomputed on stronger machines with the same model tag, prompt, temperature 0 and seed 42, and the resulting cache and graph are copied to the laptop. We say this on stage. The same inputs give the same cache entries (determinism is checked by `merge_extract_cache.py verify` on the partial cache); the laptop does not run extraction during the talk. Whether the competition allows precomputing on other hardware was asked of the organizers (ROADMAP); the answer will be written here. **32. Why do you not let the model write the answer? Why extractive?**
Because the 4B model on this laptop was both slow and unreliable at writing. On the 12 control questions through the real API the generative path took 45.7-140.8 s per model-calling question, declined even with the answer in the prompt (g001, g007 became "closest passages"), and turned two refusals (g056, g057) into passage displays (`api12-20261027-generative.md`). In extractive mode the model only picks one or two numbered sentences from the retrieved text (JSON-schema enum); the answer is those sentences verbatim, 8.1-20.2 s per question, g001 answered, g047/g056/g057 refused (`api12-20261027-extractive.md`; ADR-0009). Cost: answers are quotes, not prose, and g007/g016 are still refused. n=12, one run, no human judging. [BUILT; pilot]

**33. Why curated facts? Is the Wisteria finding real or staged?**
The finding is real in the sense that the rule engine really runs R1 over the graph and the MCP tool, and it really returns `violated`, marked conditional. The input facts are **curated**: five person-place-time statements in `data/curated/presence_demo.jsonl` (two for Wisteria Lodge, three for the Boscombe control), each with a verbatim quote that the loader checks against the book text, entered by the team with AI assistance; a human re-check has not happened. We use them because the small models do not resolve "he" and "there" on these passages (question 34). The answer, the Why panel and the slide say "curated". It shows what the rules do when the facts are right, not what the model finds alone. [BUILT; ADR-0011; human re-check TBD]

**34. What is automatic and what is curated?**
Automatic: retrieval, routing, the gate, the sentence selection, the generic extraction (events, claims, entities with quotes), the repair of its fields, the graph build and the rules. Curated: only the five presence facts above. Measured on real automatic data: the first real run on wisteria-lodge gave 107 events and 137 claims, 0 exact times, 0 story days and 1 usable presence proposition; R1 examined 19 pairs and could not decide 16, so R1/R3 find nothing automatically yet. The raw model output had participants empty in 178/178 events and `deny` polarity in 98% of claims; a deterministic repair fixes participants and polarity but cannot invent times (ADR-0012, `docs/symbolic-layer.md` 11.2). The targeted presence pass on the three c001 chunks: presence-1 failed (pronouns); presence-2.1 with candidate lists gave qwen3:4b-instruct 7 facts, 1 kept, qwen3:8b 15 facts, 11 kept, with the key fact right and several wrong persons or places (counts reported by the team, not in a committed result file). **35. Is the 4B model really that fragile?**
On the selection prompt, yes: a rewording of one clause flipped g001 from the right sentence to NONE (commits ba6f773 and 40ee6c3), so the tested wording is frozen and any change needs a re-run of the 12 questions. Also the same model chose NONE for g007 and g016, which have an answer in the corpus. We report that as a limit, not as noise.
